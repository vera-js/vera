play lab — who owns the clock under load?

Both boxes run the IDENTICAL journey — same keyframes, same explicit ease: 'linear', same play: 1 — through the real engine. The left one's clock is a CSS transition (the compositor); the right one carries a progress rider, which keeps its clock on the JS ramp. Scrolled forward they should be indistinguishable; the differences you are here to judge appear in exactly two places: toggle mid-flight (~60%) for the reversal semantics, and drag the jank slider up for who owns the clock under load.

CSS

transition-play — the compositor's clock. Its reversal follows the platform's reversing algorithm: RETIMED, so it comes home noticeably faster than it went out.

RAMP

ramp-play — the JS ramp's clock (the progress rider needs the per-frame number, which is what keeps it here). Reversal is proportional — constant speed both ways, the ramp lab's candidate B. The asymmetry between the lanes on reversal is real and is THE decision.

what to feel for, and what this decides 
1. UNDER JANK: the CSS lane should stay smooth at max slider — the compositor
   does not care about the main thread. The ramp lane stutters WITH the page.
2. REVERSAL: toggle at ~60%. CSS reverses on the platform's retiming
   (noticeably quicker home); the ramp reverses at constant speed.
   The decider question: is that asymmetry acceptable as the price of
   compositor-owned plays, or does it read as two materials?
3. Both lanes are REAL emission — inspect the elements: the CSS lane carries
   data-vm-armed + transition longhands, the ramp lane carries @keyframes
